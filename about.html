About

We know what it's like to need a place.

Same Streets was born from a simple belief: music can make a place feel like home.

Our story

Every scene starts somewhere.

Growing up in Cleveland, Tennessee, it wasn't always easy to find a place for the kind of music we loved.

The bands were out there. The people were out there. But finding each other, finding somewhere to play, and finding a place to belong wasn't always easy.

Music became more than something to listen to. It became a way to connect, to express things that were hard to put into words, and to discover who we were.

And somewhere along the way, the shows became just as important as the songs.

The crowded rooms. The voices singing back. The strangers who didn't feel like strangers by the end of the night.

Same Streets Presents exists because we want more people to experience that.

We want artists to have opportunities to be heard, communities to have places to gather, and audiences to experience shows they'll remember long after the lights come on.

We're starting in Cleveland.

But the idea is bigger than one city, one genre, or one room.

We want to make more room for music. Everywhere we can.

What we believe

The room belongs to everyone.

Music is connection.

A song can make you feel understood before you've ever met the person who wrote it.

Artists need opportunity.

Every artist deserves a chance to grow, to be seen, and to find the audience that's been waiting for them.

Shows should mean something.

The best live experiences don't just entertain us. They stay with us.

Scenes are built together.

Artists, venues, photographers, promoters, and fans all have a part to play. The scene belongs to the people who build it.

The philosophy

In the pit, as in life.

What we do here follows us.

Look out for each other. Make room for someone new. If someone falls, pick them up.

The things that make a great music community aren't so different from the things that make us better people.

The pit is more than chaos. At its best, it's a place where we let go, look out for each other, and find something bigger than ourselves.

That's the kind of community we want to build.
